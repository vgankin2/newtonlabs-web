How it works

Receipt to settled in four steps.

1
Add the items

Point your camera at the receipt and TabCut reads every line item in seconds — name, price, quantity. All processing happens on your iPhone; nothing is uploaded. Prefer to type? Manual entry works too.

2
Add your group

Type in names or pull directly from your Contacts. Everyone at the table gets their own column from the start.

3
Assign & finalize

Tap to assign dishes to whoever ordered them. Split shared items across multiple people. Add tax, tip, and the venue name — then confirm and you're done.

4
Share & collect

TabCut generates a clean, formatted summary showing what each person owes. Share it by text or email, or tap to request payment on Venmo, PayPal, or Cash App.

Features

Everything you need at the table.

👥
Add your group

Type names directly or import from your Contacts. Each person gets their own running total as items are assigned — no confusion at the end.

📷
Receipt Scanner

Camera-powered OCR reads your receipt instantly. Works on crumpled paper, dim lighting, and long itemized bills. Prefer to type? Add items manually with the built-in keyboard entry.

✂️
Item-by-item splitting

Assign each dish to the person who ordered it. No more rounding errors or awkward "can you cover the extra $2" conversations.

🤝
Split shared items

Shared appetizers and bottles split evenly across however many people ordered them — one tap per person.

💸
Share & collect payment

Send each person's total by text or email, or tap to open a payment request in Venmo, PayPal, or Cash App with the amount pre-filled.

🕐
Split History

Every split is saved. Browse past restaurant bills, see who was there, and reference old totals anytime.

🔁
Repeat a split

Go to the same spot every week? Start a new split from a previous one — same people, same structure, new amounts.

🔒
Processed on your iPhone

Receipt scanning happens entirely on-device using Apple's built-in Vision framework. Your receipt photos are never uploaded to a server — not ours, not anyone's.
