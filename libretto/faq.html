Frequently asked questions

Everything you need to know about organizing and launching prompts with Libretto. These are the same answers as Help & FAQ inside the app.

Folders and tags
What is the difference between a folder and a tag?

A folder is where a prompt lives. Each prompt is in exactly one folder, like Work or Writing, and new ones wait in the Inbox. A tag says what a prompt is about, and a prompt can carry as many as you like, like email or weekly. Use folders to file prompts away and tags to find them across folders.

How do I move a prompt to another folder?

Press and hold the prompt and choose Move to, or pick a folder in Edit prompt. To move several at once, choose Select, tick the prompts, then Move to….

You can also drag: open Folders with the folder button beside the search field, expand a folder, and drag a prompt onto another of your folders. Starters can’t be moved, so duplicate a starter to move your copy.

How many tags can a prompt have?

As many as you like. Add them in Edit prompt. To see every prompt with a tag, tap the tag button next to the search field and choose it.

Where do I rename or delete folders and tags?

Open Settings > Library and tap Folders or Tags. You can rename, recolor, reorder, merge (tags) and delete there. The same screens open from the folder button beside the search field and from Manage tags in the tag menu. Deleting a folder or tag never deletes your prompts.

Basics
What is the Inbox?

Every prompt you create without choosing a folder lands in the Inbox. It is a holding spot: open the Inbox pill, then move prompts into folders when you have a moment. The badge counts the prompts still waiting, and you can turn it off in Settings > Library.

How do templates and variables work?

Wrap the part of a prompt that changes in double curly braces, like {{topic}}. When you launch or insert the prompt, Libretto asks you to fill in each variable first. Use the same name twice and you fill it in once.

Why did the chat app open empty?

Some chat apps can’t open with text already filled in, and a very long prompt is too long to send that way. The prompt is always copied first, so just paste it into the message box. If you would rather every app opened empty, turn off “Open with the prompt filled in” in Settings > Launch. The default chat app is set there too.

How is “Used” counted?

Each time you send a prompt to a chat app, or copy it, Libretto adds one to its Used count and moves it to your Recent list. Inserting from the keyboard counts too when Full Access is on.

How do I hide or restore starter prompts?

Swipe a starter and tap Hide, or open it and use its menu. Bring them back one at a time from Settings > Library > Hidden starters, or restore them all from the View menu. To turn every starter off, switch off Show starter prompts in Settings > Library.

Tips and gestures
Which swipes and shortcuts are there?

In the List and Compact views, swipe a prompt right to pin or unpin it, and left to delete it (on a starter, to hide it). A bar lets you undo for a few seconds. Press and hold a prompt for Launch, Copy, Share, Edit, Duplicate, Pin to top, Move to, Select and Delete.

In Settings > Tags, swipe left to delete, swipe right to merge, or press and hold to rename, merge or delete.

Can I highlight the variables in the keyboard?

Yes. While you fill in a prompt’s variables on the keyboard, press and hold the variable strip to switch the highlight on or off.

Keyboard
How do I add the keyboard?

Tap Add the keyboard in Settings > Keyboard, then Open Settings. It opens on Libretto’s page: tap Keyboards, switch on Libretto, then come back to the app. Allow Full Access is optional.

You can also add it by hand in iOS Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards > Add New Keyboard… > Libretto. To type with it, tap a text field, press and hold the globe key and choose Libretto.

Do I need to allow Full Access?

No. Full Access is optional. Your prompts show up and insert without it. Turn it on only if you want the keyboard to keep your Recent list and Used counts up to date. Libretto never collects what you type. See the Privacy Policy.

The keyboard doesn’t show my prompts

Open Libretto once so it can share your prompts with the keyboard; it updates whenever you change something. Also check that Libretto is switched on in Settings > General > Keyboard > Keyboards. If the Starters pill is missing, see Settings > Keyboard > Show Starters pill.

Why can’t I use it in password fields?

iOS switches to its own keyboard in password and other secure fields, for every third-party keyboard. It is an iOS rule, not a Libretto setting.

Sync, privacy and Pro
Does Libretto work on iPad?

Libretto is designed for iPhone. You can install it on an iPad and use it there in a phone-sized window, but it has no iPad layout, so it is best on an iPhone. Your prompts sync between your devices through iCloud either way.

Are my prompts backed up?

Libretto syncs your prompts through your private iCloud, so they follow you to your other devices and come back if you reinstall. You need to be signed in to iCloud; Settings > Sync shows the status.

Does Libretto see my prompts?

No. Libretto has no account and no server that stores your prompts. They live on your device and in your own iCloud, which only you can reach. When you open a prompt in an AI app, that app receives it, under its own privacy policy. Read ours in full on the Privacy Policy page.

What does Pro add?

Pro adds themes, typography choices, more Library backgrounds, Prompt Cleanup, version history and Home Screen and Lock Screen widgets. Everything else, including the keyboard, stays free. Pro is a one-time purchase, not a subscription.

What do I need for Prompt Cleanup?

Prompt Cleanup is part of Pro. It runs on your iPhone with Apple Intelligence, so it needs a supported iPhone running iOS 26 or later. It fixes typos, trims filler and tightens a messy prompt, and you choose whether to use the result.

How do I go back to an earlier version of a prompt?

With Pro, open the prompt, tap the three dots and choose Version history. Libretto keeps the last 25 versions of each prompt, saved whenever you change its title or text. Tap one to see what changed, then copy its text or restore it. Restoring saves your current text first, so you can always undo it. Versions are only saved while Pro is on.

How do I add a widget?

With Pro, touch and hold your Home Screen, tap Edit, then Add Widget and search for Libretto. For the Lock Screen, touch and hold it, tap Customize and add Libretto under the clock. To choose what a widget shows (Pinned, Recent, Inbox, a folder, or one prompt), touch and hold it and tap Edit Widget. Widgets show titles only and open the prompt when you tap it. Built-in starters are not shown; duplicate one into your library to feature it. If a widget looks out of date, open Libretto once.
